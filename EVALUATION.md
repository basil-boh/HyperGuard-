# HyperGuard — Evaluation Options

Design menu, not an implementation. Every option below states the claim it tests, the
**baseline it is measured against**, the metric, and — importantly — what it *cannot* prove.

Nothing here is worth building until the trap in §0 is dealt with.

---

## 0. The trap: your current evaluation is circular

Before any of this, understand why `seed.py` passing proves nothing about accuracy.

The victim's replies come from `scam_taxonomy.victim_script(archetype)`. The Educator then
matches those sentences against `archetype.indicators`. Both lists live in the same file,
written by the same hand.

> The classifier is being tested on sentences generated from the answer key.
> It scores 100% and always will, including if you deleted half the logic.

**Every option below is void unless the test data is generated blind** — by a person or model
that has never seen `scam_taxonomy.py`. This is the single most important design constraint on
the whole exercise. If you take one thing from this document, take this.

The same applies to the seeded personas: `Wong Ah Kow → 99% critical` is a property of the
constants in `seed_data.py`, not evidence the risk engine works.

## 1. What needs a number

Ranked by how badly the absence hurts you, and by how much a judge or a bank would press.

| # | Claim you make | Currently backed by | Consequence if false |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "It only engages on genuine anomalies" | nothing | The product is unshippable. A bank will not deploy an unmeasured false-positive rate. |
| 2 | The conversation changes the outcome | nothing | The entire voice layer — your differentiator — is decoration. |
| 3 | The Educator identifies the scam | circular test | Your second agent is unproven. |
| 4 | Scoring against *her* baseline beats a global rule | nothing | The Digital Twin's whole thesis. |
| 5 | Decisions are explainable | asserted | Compliance blocker, and it's cheap to test. |
| 6 | Degrades gracefully without credentials | `seed.py` runs | Mild. Already semi-demonstrated. |
| 7 | Intervenes inside 60 seconds | asserted | Easy to measure, easy win. |

## 2. The dataset (prerequisite for options A–D)

Roughly **500 cases**, and the negatives matter more than the positives.

| Slice | n | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Scam transcripts, 6 archetypes | 180 (30/class) | SPF annual scam briefs, ScamShield advisories, FTC/ACCC case write-ups, r/Scams posts. Public, real, and none of it written by you. |
| Adversarial paraphrases | 60 | Same scams, reworded to avoid your indicator strings. Generated by a model **given the scam narrative but not the indicator list**. |
| Legitimate-but-suspicious | 180 | The ones that will hurt: an 81-year-old wiring SGD 12,000 to a new payee for a *contractor deposit*, a *medical bill*, a *property deposit*, a *grandchild's wedding*. Same surface features as a scam, benign cause. |
| Legitimate-and-obvious | 80 | Groceries, recurring bills. Should never trip anything. |

**Labelling:** two independent annotators, report Cohen's κ. If κ < 0.7 your labels aren't
real and neither are your numbers.

**Cost:** this is the expensive part — 2–4 days, mostly sourcing and labelling. Everything
downstream is cheap once it exists. There is no shortcut that isn't circular.

## Option A — Risk engine, against four baselines

**Claim:** personalised behavioural scoring beats what banks do now.
**Effort:** 1 day on top of the dataset.

Run all five scorers over the same cases:

| | Scorer | What it represents |
|---|---|---|
| B0 | Approve everything | The actual status quo for authorised payments |
| B1 | Flag amount > SGD 5,000 | The naive rule |
| B2 | Population z-score (σ across the whole book) | A bank's non-personalised anomaly rule |
| B3 | `new_payee` alone | Your single strongest signal, unaided |
| **B4** | **Full logistic model** | HyperGuard |
| B5 | LLM given raw txn + history, no features | "Why not just prompt it?" |

**Metrics:** ROC-AUC and PR-AUC (PR matters more — scams are rare); TPR/FPR **at your actual
0.58 threshold**, not at the optimum; a calibration curve (does 0.7 mean 70%?); and a
threshold sweep so you can show the operating curve rather than one cherry-picked point.

**The three comparisons that matter:**
- **B4 vs B2** — if personalisation doesn't win, the Digital Twin thesis is dead.
- **B4 vs B3** — if the full model doesn't beat one rule, the other seven signals are theatre.
- **B4 vs B5** — if a plain LLM matches you, your feature engineering needs a different defence
  (it has one: explainability and determinism — but you'd need to say that instead).

**Bonus, near-free:** ablate each signal, re-run, report the AUC drop per signal. That turns
your asserted weights (`new_payee = 1.7`) into measured ones, and tells you which to delete.

**Cannot prove:** that the threshold is *right*. That's a business decision about the cost of a
false block vs a missed scam — see Option C.

## Option B — Educator, keyword vs LLM, including adversarial

**Claim:** the Educator identifies which scam script is being run.
**Effort:** half a day on top of the dataset. **Highest insight per hour of any option here.**

| | Classifier |
|---|---|
| C0 | Majority class |
| **C1** | **Current keyword matcher** |
| C2 | LLM zero-shot, 6 archetypes + "none" |
| C3 | LLM with keyword matcher as a strictness floor (the proposed design) |

**Metrics:** per-archetype precision/recall/F1, macro-F1, full confusion matrix, and
**abstention quality** — how often does it correctly return `none` on the legitimate slice?
That last one is where a keyword matcher usually bleeds, and it maps directly to false alarms
on a real call.

**Run every classifier twice: clean set, then adversarial set.** The delta is the finding.

> Expected result, stated in advance so you can't rationalise afterwards: C1 scores respectably
> on clean data and **falls off a cliff on paraphrases**, because `"safe account"` is a string
> match and *"an account where it'll be protected"* isn't. C2/C3 should hold. If that's what
> happens, you have a measured, quotable argument for the LLM upgrade instead of a hunch — and
> a great slide.

Also report **confidence calibration**. Your formula is `0.4 + 0.18 × hits`, capped at 0.98.
Plot claimed confidence against observed accuracy. If a "0.9" is right 60% of the time, the
number shouldn't be shown to an operator, and the `>= 0.6` routing gate in `graph.py` is
tuned on a meaningless scale.

**Cannot prove:** that naming the script changes victim behaviour. That's Option F.

## Option C — Does the conversation actually change anything?

**Claim:** intervening *during* beats detecting *after*. This is the thesis of the product.
**Effort:** 1 day. **This is the experiment I'd want if I could only run one.**

Four pipelines, same cases, same threshold:

| | Pipeline |
|---|---|
| E0 | Approve everything |
| E1 | Block on amount threshold, no call |
| **E2** | **Risk score only, no conversation** ← what your live path does today |
| **E3** | **Full swarm with the call** |

**Metric:** don't report accuracy. Report **expected cost** under an explicit, stated cost
matrix — e.g. a missed scam costs the transfer amount, a false block costs SGD 40 of handling
plus a churn probability. Then sweep the cost ratio and show where each pipeline wins. This is
the language a bank makes decisions in, and it forces the asymmetry into the open.

**E2 vs E3 is the whole question.** If the conversation flips no decisions, your five-agent
swarm is an expensive risk score, and you should find that out privately rather than on stage.

> Note the current blocker: because of the block-reversal gap, **E3 cannot currently beat E2 in
> the direction that matters** — the call can only add blocks, never release. Fixing that
> (P1 item 6 in `FUNCTIONALITY.md`) is a *prerequisite* for this experiment to be meaningful,
> not a follow-up to it.

## Option D — Explainability, scored rather than asserted

**Claim:** "explainable, audited decisions."
**Effort:** half a day.

Two ways, pick either:

1. **Signal fidelity (cheap, objective).** Your dataset labels *why* each case is a scam. Check
   whether the fired signals match the labelled cause. Metric: precision/recall over reasons.
   Catches the failure where the model is right for the wrong reason.
2. **Human comprehension (slower, more convincing).** Show 20 rationales to 5 people who don't
   know the system; ask them to predict the verdict and name the driving factor. Baseline: the
   same cases with only the score shown. Metric: agreement rate and time-to-answer.

**Cannot prove:** regulatory sufficiency. Nobody can, without a regulator.

## Option E — System behaviour (cheap, do these regardless)

**Effort:** a few hours each, and they all belong in CI.

- **Time-to-intervention.** Wall-clock from transfer submit → first ring, p50/p95. Your claim is
  a 60-second window; measure where you land in it. Baseline: a bank's existing manual fraud
  callback (hours), and an SMS OTP (seconds, no conversation). You'll win this one — take it.
- **Degradation matrix.** Run the eval set with each capability toggled off (LLM, telephony,
  speech, persistence). Report the quality drop per capability. This converts "graceful
  degradation" from a design story into a table, and it's the kind of table that reads as
  engineering maturity.
- **Property tests on the risk engine** (Hypothesis): score always in [0,1]; monotone —
  increasing the amount never decreases the score; deterministic across runs; no signal fires
  without a non-empty reason string.
- **Regression tests for the two known bugs**, so they can't come back: a case persists with a
  scenario persona (the FK bug), and a "clear" follow-up assessment releases a blocked transfer
  (the reversal gap). Write these as failing tests *first* — they document the bugs precisely.
- **Contract tests** on the API so the console and mobile can't silently drift.
- **Adversarial webhook test:** POST a forged answer to `/twilio/voice/answer` with a guessed
  case ID and assert it's rejected. Today it isn't — this test fails until signature validation
  lands.

## Option F — Does it actually stop a person? (the honest ceiling)

**Claim:** naming the scam script breaks the manipulation.
**Effort:** 1–2 weeks, needs ethics care. **Not for the hackathon.**

Everything above measures whether the *system* is right. None of it measures whether a
frightened 72-year-old, mid-manipulation, *listens*. That is the actual product claim.

Realistic version: a scripted role-play study, 20–30 participants, randomised between a generic
warning ("this may be a scam") and HyperGuard's named-script counter-line. Measure whether they
proceed. Baseline is the generic warning, because that's what banks say today.

**Say this limitation out loud rather than waiting to be asked.** "We can show the system is
accurate; we haven't yet shown it's persuasive, and here's the study that would" is a much
stronger position than being caught without it.

---

## Recommendation

**If you do three things:** the dataset (§2), **Option B** (keyword vs LLM, adversarial), and
**Option C** (does the conversation change anything). Roughly a week. Option B gives you a
memorable slide; Option C tells you whether the product is what you think it is.

**If you do one thing:** Option B. It's half a day past the dataset, it directly tests the
weakest real component, and the adversarial delta is the most quotable number you can produce.

**Do Option E's property and regression tests regardless** — they're hours, they belong in CI,
and two of them pin bugs that are live right now.

**Sequencing note:** fix block-reversal *before* Option C, or the experiment measures a
pipeline that structurally cannot exonerate anyone.

---

## What none of this can tell you

- Whether real scam narratives in the wild resemble your six archetypes. Your taxonomy could be
  precise and complete against your dataset and still miss the scam that's trending next month.
- Whether it works in Mandarin, Hokkien, Malay or Tamil — the languages a meaningful share of
  the target users would actually panic in.
- Whether it survives the scammer being *on the other line*, coaching the victim through your
  call. Nothing in the system models an adversary who adapts.
- Whether a bank would accept the liability of an automated block.

Naming these unprompted is worth more than another point of F1.
